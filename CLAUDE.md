# The Moth House

A point-and-click adventure for children: a warm, cosy mystery that works as a first taste of modern point-and-click games, while still satisfying grown-up puzzle fans. Players choose one of three storylines, Mary's (10), Elliot's (7) or Zaina's (4). Each follows the same summer, from the last day of school in Singapore, through packing and the flight, to Nana and Jedi's in Enfield, Trent Park and the hidden Moth House, with rooms and puzzles made for that child's age. The three stories meet in a shared finale. All characters, art and story are original — never borrow names, characters or imagery from any other game.

**The story plan lives in `STORY.md`, and the building rules in `next/PLAYBOOK.md`.** Read both before designing or building any room; the playbook holds every rule and every piece of the owner's feedback, and is the standard each room must meet. Build new rooms from it, update it when the owner changes the story, and never fill in its "TO DECIDE" items without asking him.

**`next/CHECKLIST.md` is the owner's feedback turned into checks.** Read it at the start of any update and mark the lines the work touches; walk it over every amended or new thing, close up at the owner's screen shape, before showing it; run `node tools/audit.js` (with the local server up) before every commit and fix what it reports. A fault the owner reports gets reproduced, confirmed in words, fixed at its cause, and added to the checklist.

**The owner's game design library is the third reference for designing puzzles.** It lives in his private repo `me565/core-skills`, folder `game-design/` (attach that repo, read-only, if it isn't in the session). It holds the mechanics he likes from games he has reviewed, each written as a general rule with examples (`mechanics/`), the experiences he wants avoided (`avoid.md`), one review per game (`games/`), and how to use it (`README.md`). Read its README and `mechanics/_index.md` at Gate B, before drawing a puzzle chart, and draw on the mechanics marked `love` first. Two rules: where the library and this repo disagree (for example on who the game is for), stop and ask the owner rather than picking one; and never copy a reviewed game's puzzles, art, text or characters, only the idea underneath. Never edit the library from here; ideas for it go to the owner.

The owner is a non-technical but AI-savvy builder working from a Chromebook. Explain what you are about to do in plain language, and keep him in the loop before anything goes live.

## How it is hosted and shipped

- Hosted on GitHub Pages at https://me565.github.io (this repo, `me565.github.io`, branch `main`, root folder).
- Shipped to Android as a Trusted Web Activity built with PWABuilder. The Android app simply loads this website, so **anything merged to `main` is live to every player within minutes**.

## Working rules

- Never commit directly to `main`. Work on a branch, share a preview or test steps, and merge only after the owner has tested and said yes.
- **Always give the owner a fresh play link.** Every reply that builds or changes something playable ends with a githack link pinned to the new commit, in the form `https://rawcdn.githack.com/me565/me565.github.io/<full commit id>/next/landscape/index.html`. A new commit means a new address, so his tablet can never show a cached copy; never reuse a link or give the branch-name form. Bump `BUILD` in the page with each change so the menu's diagnostics line shows which version he is on. Once merged, the address is https://me565.github.io/next/landscape/. Never make him ask for it.
- Never commit the Android signing keystore, its passwords or `signing-key-info.txt`. This repo is public.
- Never delete or rename `manifest.webmanifest`, `sw.js`, the icons, or anything in `.well-known/` — the Android app depends on them.
- Keep `manifest.webmanifest` `id`, `start_url` and `scope` as `./`.
- **The game is played in landscape** (owner's brief, 28 Sept 2026, see "Visual style" in `STORY.md`): scenes are 16:9 with the inventory slots in a strip along the bottom. The current `next/` engine is still portrait (viewBox 360×480) and needs converting; the live game is portrait too. Tap targets large enough for fingers, no hover-only interactions.
- The whole game must always fit the screen, with no vertical or horizontal scrolling, on any phone, tablet or desktop window. If a phone is held in portrait, ask the player to turn it rather than squeezing the scene.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Child-appropriate: cosy mystery, never frightening. Nothing unsafe to copy (no matches, fire, knives, dangerous climbing, going into water). No failing and no timers; wrong tries get a friendly line.
- Any clue given by sound must also be shown visually.
- Before putting real Trent Park or Enfield history into the game, check the facts and tell the owner what you checked.
- Legal safety: follow "Fiction and legal safety" in `STORY.md`. Use the fictional school names (Knightsbrook International School, Little Waves Kindergarten), never the real ones. No real brands, logos or airline names, and never the Merlion, which is a protected symbol in Singapore. Keep the fiction notice in the game's credits.

## Level-building gates (owner, 17 Oct 2026)

Every level is built in the order below, and the record of it lives in `levels/LEVEL-<key>.md`, made from `levels/LEVEL-TEMPLATE.md`. Read that file before any work on the level. The rules here override anything else in this document that would let a step be skipped.

**The rule that matters most: nothing that spends API credit is made until the level file shows the gates before it signed off by the owner.** That means no call to the image model, the speech model or Lyria for a level until its file has `Signed off:` lines, with a date, under gates A, B, C and D (and E for the batch). A sign-off is the owner's own words in the file, added by the owner or at the owner's clear instruction in chat ("sign off gate B"). Never write a sign-off line on your own judgement, and never treat silence as approval.

The stages and their gates:

1. **Brief** → *Gate A, story.* Setting, child and age, objective, difficulty, signature moment. Checked against `STORY.md`, the age, and the fiction and legal rules.
2. **Game map** and 3. **Puzzle chart** → *Gate B, level outline.* Rooms, views, doors, what starts locked and what opens it, drawn as a floor plan with `tools/levelmap.py` from `levels/LEVEL-<key>-map.json` (the owner's style, `next/PLAYBOOK.md` 4y); then every trail, item, clue, lock and tiered hint as one dependency chart. Paper-played from the chart before it is shown: every answer has an in-game clue, no puzzle type used more than twice, no clue shared with another child's story.
4. **Greybox** → *Gate C, it plays right.* The whole level playable with plain labelled boxes and the real puzzle logic, hints and saves. No generated art, no recorded voice. Fresh play link, scripted playthrough, audit. The owner plays it on the tablet. A fault here is fixed here; it is far cheaper than after the art exists.
5. **Asset and script list** → *Gate D, list and budget.* Every picture, voice line and track the level needs, each with its prompt, checked against the asset register (reuse before making), with a credit estimate. The script is locked here: a voice line changed after this point is a re-record, and is noted as one.
6a. **Sample pack** → *Gate E, samples.* The hardest wall, one cut-out, one close-up, two or three voice lines spanning hint, story and wrong-try, and about thirty seconds of the track. Dropped into the greybox and judged there, on the tablet, never as loose files. A failed sample goes back to its prompt, not to the batch.
6b. **Batch.** Everything else, made to match the signed-off samples. Originals (the 2K PNGs and raw audio) are copied to the owner's Drive folder for the level before the session ends; the repo keeps only the shipped sizes.
7. **Integrate and polish** → *Final sign-off.* Art placed, animation, ambient sound, comic intro, opening gesture. Full checklist walk at the owner's screen shapes, audit clean.
8. **Release.** Merge; outside playtesters; their notes logged in the level file.

Going back is normal. A gate that fails sends the work back to the stage it belongs to; it does not open a shortcut to a later stage.

Every test, at every stage, feeds the learning loop: a fault is reproduced, confirmed in words, fixed at its cause in this level, then classified. If it is a rule for every level, it is written into `PLAYBOOK.md`, as a line in `CHECKLIST.md`, and into `tools/audit.js` when a machine can check it. If it is only this level's, it goes in the level file's notes. Never skip the classification; that is how each level starts smarter than the last.

While a level is between Gate A and release, the whole-game layer is frozen: the engine, navigation, controls, saves, hint system, art style guide, the moth's voice, music themes. Ideas for it go to the backlog in `PLAYBOOK.md` and are built between levels, then tested across every level.

One level file covers one child's stage. The three children's versions of the same stage are three level files, each with its own gates and budget; they may share rooms and reuse assets, and the asset register says which.

At the end of every stage, record the credit actually spent in the level file's ledger next to the estimate. Re-dos are counted separately, with their reason, because they are what the loop is trying to reduce.

## What is live now

Since 30 Sept 2026 (pull request #3 and the root swap that followed) the live game is the landscape children's version in `next/landscape/`: the root page sends players there. The original single room, written for adults, stays at `parlour.html`; this section describes that room's code. Chapter 2 in `STORY.md` lists what changes when this room is rebuilt (matches become a winding handle, the porcelain face becomes a paint pot, Ada becomes the girl in the portrait); the ring lock and the looking-glass clock stay.

Setting: a Victorian parlour. Opening line: you wake with a note pinned to your sleeve — "return what this house has lost".

Puzzle chain:
1. Rug → lift corner → matchbox.
2. Matchbox on oil lamp → room lights up.
3. Bell jar (only once lit) → moth flies to the lamp and circles it anticlockwise ("always backwards, like a clock unwinding"); four shadows appear on the wall in a ring.
4. Wall ring: hand above, eye right, key below, moon left. Drawer lock (4 dials cycling moon/eye/key/feather/hand, checked only when you tap "Pull the drawer") is engraved "Begin with what the moth wears. Then follow it." → start at the eye (the moth's wings have eyes), go anticlockwise like the moth → eye, hand, moon, key → clock hands.
5. Interaction model: tap an object to look; tap an inventory item to select it, then tap where it goes. Wrong item gives "The X does not belong there." Messages appear in the `#msg` line under the scene. Close-ups (drawer lock, clock) are overlays inside `.stage`, with their own feedback line; neither checks the answer until the player commits (Pull the drawer / Let it run), so answers cannot be found by watching for a click.
