# The Moth House — room-building playbook

The working rules for every room in the landscape game, distilled from the owner's brief and his feedback on the classroom test (28 Sept 2026). Read this before touching `next/landscape/` or generating any art. `STORY.md` says *what* each room contains; this says *how* it is built and what "good" looks like. Every rule here came from a real problem, so none is optional.



## 1. Layout and screen

- Landscape only. The scene is 16:9; the inventory is a strip of ten slots under it, always visible. Held in portrait on a phone, ask the player to turn it; never squeeze the scene.
- Nothing scrolls, ever: not the page, not a close-up, not the inventory. Every close-up card is sized from the scene's height (the timetable card is 60 units wide so its 230:176 shape fits) and has `overflow:hidden`. Test at phone (844×390), tablet (1180×820) and large (2000×1200) sizes.
- Turn arrows sit halfway up the left and right edges. The room label is top-left, hint (moth) and menu top-right. Keep the middle of each side edge bare in the art.
- The message line is a translucent band at the bottom of the scene; it hides while a close-up is open.
- Close-ups bring the object forward over the room, which is blurred and dimmed, **including the cut-out layers** (blur `#hots`, not just the wall). Tapping anywhere outside the card, or on the dark margin, steps back; there is no × button; a small "Tap anywhere to step back" caption sits under the card.
- A build id (`BUILD`) appears in the menu's diagnostics line with sound state, music state, reduce-motion and screen size. Bump it with every change. Saves are keyed by build, so every build starts fresh; "Start again" is in the menu.



## 2. Art pipeline (Gemini through the key in the environment)

- Walls, from 21 Oct 2026 (4aa): GPT Image 2.5 Sunburst through OpenRouter on the GitHub runner (`evals/images/`), with Nano Banana 2 as the fallback; one room specification per room (`evals/images/rooms/<room>.json`) from which `room_prompts.py` builds every wall's prompt and `layout.py` draws a wordless layout sketch per wall; the anchor wall first, then its neighbours with the anchor attached. The style sentence is the rendered one signed off that day (below), not the flat one in `art/classroom/PROMPTS.md`, which stays as the record of the walls made before. Sparse and purposeful: no clutter that is not a puzzle, a hint or a thing a child would poke.
- **The style, signed off by the owner on 21 Oct 2026** ("sign off the style", after marking 56 evaluation pictures): *a polished, finished illustration drawn for a game, not a picture book for toddlers; clean dark-ink outlines on everything, a shadow side on every object and soft cast shadows from one light source, so things sit solidly on their surfaces; smooth colour with gentle shading, a faint paper-grain texture and a gentle vignette at the edges; bright and clear, not hazy or dark.* It replaces "clean, calm flat illustration … flat colour fills, very little shading": his marks went to rendered pictures with shadows and finishing, and the flattest got the lowest.
- Consistency: generate one wall alone, then give the others a crop of it (objects cut off) as a style reference, with "copy only its style, not its objects".
- Never mention the arrows or the interface in a prompt (the model draws them). Walls made before 17 Oct 2026 were prompted as a "shallow box with narrow side walls"; new walls are prompted flat and the engine paints the side walls from the neighbouring views (4z). Never draw boxes, panels or placeholder shapes.
- Anything that will move, be picked up, or change state is **not drawn on the wall**. Describe its place as empty ("the purple locker has no door", "the end of the cabinet is empty") and generate the thing separately as a cut-out. This avoids the edit-and-drift cycle (editing a finished wall shifts everything slightly and every position must be re-measured).
- Cut-outs: 1:1 or 3:4, 1K, "alone on a pure flat white background, same style as the attached crop, no surface, no shadow, no frame, no text". Flood-fill the white away from the edges (so white inside the object survives), then clear any enclosed near-white region that is not part of the thing (a handle's loop, a key's hole, a gap between leaves: the owner's recording, 24 Oct 2026, MOTH-70), crop to content, quantise to 256 colours. Give the model a crop of the object from the wall when one exists. The rim is keyed soft (24 Oct 2026, MOTH-85): every opaque pixel within two of transparency takes its alpha from its whiteness and has the white pulled out of its colour, or a pale fringe shows against every dark surface (the ruler in the drawer).
- Inventory icons are made the same way (on white, keyed out), never small vector drawings.
- Text in generated art is unreliable: keep lettering to short labels (SUPPLIES, 5H, LOST PROPERTY). Anything the puzzle depends on (the timetable grid) is drawn by the game over the picture so it exactly matches its close-up.
- Save walls at 1920×1080 JPEG quality 88; keep the 2K PNG originals out of the repo.
- Photos of real places the owner sends are reference, never source: they are not kept in the repo and no face, name, crest, logo or uniform in them is reproduced. What is kept is `next/art/REFERENCE.md`, a word picture of the place with the must-not-copy items marked; prompts draw on it at Gate D (owner's photos of the real school, 17 Oct 2026).



## 3. Placement and measurement

- Positions are picture percentages. Measure them on the final wall with a 5% grid (1% for fiddly things like a box rim), never by eye from a thumbnail.
- A cut-out's box must keep the picture's own proportions: `h = w × (pictureH / pictureW) × 16/9`. A wrong box squashes the cut-out and makes it float (the bin lid, the mug).
- Things that stand on a surface sit exactly on it (the apple on the books, the mug on the desk, the pots on the sill) and get a soft contact shadow (`shadow`). Nothing floats.
- Small tap targets are placed above large ones (the engine sorts by area), so the mug on the desk stays tappable.
- Hotspots may be bigger than the object for fingers, but never so big they steal a neighbour's tap.



## 4. Animation ("physics")

- Every moving thing is its own cut-out with a transparent edge, over a wall it is absent from. **Never shift a rectangle of the background**: it reads as a shaking sticker. This was the single biggest complaint.
- Movement is organic and drawn in slices on a canvas: a plant bends more at the tip than the base (`bend`), a coat swings from its hook with the hem trailing (`swing`), paper lifts and settles (`lift`), hard things rock on their base (`wobble`), soft things squash and lean from the base and settle (`slump`: a bag, a cushion; never `wobble`, which swivels), cupboard doors open a crack on their hinges (`peek`), a lid tips (`flip`), things in a box jump up and drop back (`pop`), a small animal breathes and twitches (`sniff`). Only the part that can move moves: a cut-out that stands in or on something carries `pin`, the share of its height from the bottom that stays still (the pot under a plant, measured from the cut-out's own rim), and the movement starts above it. A thing behind `glass` is multiplied with the wall's glass, not veiled by it, so it stays solid and the bars still sit in front (owner, 22 Oct 2026, MOTH-46 to 48).
- Only the part that would really move moves: a plant bends, its pot never does (split the cut-out at the rim; draw the pot in front). A hamster does not leap about a cage; a subtle sniff is more believable.
- Physical sense must hold: a coat hung by its collar loop shows its back (hood outward, no buttons). A hood on the viewer's side with buttons on the viewer's side is impossible.
- Occlusion is done with real pieces, not clip lines: the front of the lost-property box is cut from the wall picture and drawn over the things inside it, so they are genuinely behind it. A straight clip line looked wrong.
- Things behind glass are clipped to the glass, drawn duller (`tone`), and the wall's glass is painted faintly over them **in every frame of the animation** (the glass must be painted after the drawing origin is reset).
- Light: a cut-out on a lit wall takes the wall's light and shade behind it, as brightness only, laid over it (`lit`), so a sunbeam falls across a coat.
- Most tappable things move; the ones that don't (a whiteboard, a radiator, a window) still make a sound and say a line. A door knob that cannot be cut out cleanly stays still rather than faking it.
- Under reduce-motion, tap feedback still plays, shorter and gentler; only ambient motion (the sparkle) stops.
- Some of the background is alive on its own, without a tap: only where a real thing would move, slow and small, never distracting, and never the sole carrier of a clue (a fish drifting in its tank, rain on a window, bunting stirring). Under reduce-motion it rests. Most of a room stays still; one or two living things per view is plenty (owner, 30 Sept 2026; backlog item 16).
- Built in 2026-09-30m: a layer's `idle` is `swim` (the layer's box is the water; `size` is the picture's share of it, `depth` its height, `period` the crossing; it turns at each end, bobs, and sits behind `glass` like anything in a tank), `breathe` (a slow stretch from the base: Nutmeg, Peanut) or `sway` (a stir from the top, or from the bottom with `pivot:'bottom'` for balloons on strings; `phase` staggers neighbours). Weather is code-drawn: `draw:'rain'` runs faint streaks down a box the size of the pane, `draw:'steam'` lifts three wisps. One loop (`idleTick`) redraws the current view's living layers about twenty times a second and rests under a close-up, the menu, a hidden tab and reduced motion. The first living things: Captain in his tank (his drawn fish painted out of the wall), Nutmeg, Peanut, rain on the flat's three windows, steam off the toaster, the 2P balloons tied to Miss Okafor's desk, and the four paintings on Zaina's line (now cut-outs).
- Anything with an openable part (a drawer, a cupboard, a lid, a box, a wardrobe) opens and closes on a tap, before and after what was inside has been taken, with its own sound, and stays as you left it on the wall and in the close-up. An empty open drawer is still a drawer. Doorways between rooms are exempt: you walk through them (owner, 30 Sept 2026; backlog item 15).
- Tap animations must never be skipped silently on the owner's device: the tablet reports reduce-motion, and the early version showed nothing at all.



## 4a. Rooms are boxes with four sides, or rectangles

- A room has four walls. A square room is four views. A long room has two short walls and two long walls, and each long wall is two views that continue into each other: the first half shows one corner and runs off the far edge, the second half shows the other corner. Stepping between the two halves pans the picture sideways; turning a corner fades. Never draw six closed boxes and call it a room: it reads as a hexagon.
- Prompt the halves as "the LEFT half of one long wall: the left side wall visible, the back wall and floor running straight off the right edge with no corner" and the mirror for the right half. Declare the room's `long` pairs in `ROOMS` so the engine pans.
- Clues arrive in order. A book (or drawer, or box) that holds the next clue gives it up only once the previous clue has pointed at it; before that it is just a book, with a friendly line. Reading "that's all three" while missing one feels like cheating. Parallel streams are fine when the story says so; a chain is a chain.
- Things inside close-ups (books on a shelf, dials, cards) are pictures to the same standard as the walls, never flat coloured buttons. Titles are drawn by the game along the spines so the words stay exact.



## 4a-ii. Doors and rooms, the Dark Dome way

- A room is a square box of four walls. If a room needs more, split it into two rooms; never stretch a room to six views.
- A doorway you can walk through is drawn **open**, with a glimpse of the next room through it. A closed door means locked: permanently, or until a puzzle or key opens it. Tapping a closed door says so.
- Walking through zooms into the doorway and dims, then the new room settles in from slightly large. Turning a corner fades.
- Close-ups of shelves, boxes and drawers are generated pictures of the real thing, with tap targets and the game's text laid over them. Never flat buttons.
- Two typefaces in the whole game: Caprasimo for titles and menus, Atkinson Hyperlegible for everything the game says.
- Cut-outs are hardened to fully opaque or fully clear before saving (colour reduction otherwise leaves a quarter of the pixels half-transparent, which is why the trolley, lid and hat looked see-through). No sunlight overlay on small things; it reads as transparency.
- Things that turn (a globe) really turn: the map slides round the sphere under a still arc.
- On a phone or tablet in a browser, offer full screen once and keep a toggle in the menu; inside the Android app the game is already full screen.



## 4b. No empty walls

- Every wall must earn its place in the critical path or the bonus path: a clue, an item, a hint-giver, a door, or a piece of the puzzle's information. A wall that is only atmosphere is wasted time; the owner's yardstick is that in the games he loves everything feels connected. Before generating a wall, write down what it contributes.
- Split information across walls and rooms so the player has to look: the timetable is on one wall, the key to its pictures on the whiteboard on another.
- A tap on something with no clue gets one short in-page line, never a close-up. Close-ups are for reading something that matters (a note, a riddle, a key) or working something (a lock, a shelf).
- Never show technical details (build id, sound state) to a player; the menu's diagnostics line appears only with `?debug` in the address.
- Going through a door leans the picture in towards the door before the room changes, so the player sees the intent.



## 4c. Parallel trails, the Rusty Lake and Dark Dome way (owner, 29 Sept 2026)

- A stage is not a chain. The goal lock is seen early (Mary's locker, three dials, on the first corridor wall), and the envelope gives all three riddles at once, so three trails open together and can be followed in any order. The player should usually be carrying two or three half-finished things at once.
- Each trail ends in a lock that needs two different kinds of input: a found item **and** something observed or read somewhere else (the star key and the star on the STEM padlock; the beater and the order of notes on the page; the moth's stripes and the colour dials). One input alone gives a friendly "not yet" line.
- Trails cross rooms: the book in the library points to the art room, whose key opens a cabinet in the hall. Nothing is solved in the room where its clue was found.
- Inside a trail the order still holds (the key is not in the apron until the note says so; the chime bars say "nothing to strike them with" until the beater is there), because reading a clue before it has been pointed at feels like cheating. Between trails there is no order.
- Convergence takes one piece from each trail (the three slips) and one more observation (the timetable read with the whiteboard key). Optional content (the bonus riddle) never gates anything.
- The moth's hints follow the trail the player has got furthest along, so a stuck player is helped with what they are actually doing.
- Padlocks and drawers are cut-outs over a wall that has only a hasp; an opened padlock springs, falls and is gone. Inside a cabinet or cupboard is a generated close-up with the slip on its shelf; once the slip is taken the close-up is not shown again (a tap gives a line), so the picture never shows a slip the player already has.
- Anything the puzzle depends on (the coloured notes on the page, the three stripes under the moth, the colour of each dial) is drawn by the game over the pictures, so wall, close-up and inventory always agree exactly.



## 4d. Stages (29 Sept 2026)

- The engine (`next/landscape/index.html`) knows nothing about any room. Each stage is a file in `next/landscape/stages/` that calls `registerStage(key, {…})` with its rooms, walls, doors, tap targets, cut-out layers, close-up pictures to preload, notes, items, hints, item uses, `act(id)` (return `false` for a door the engine should open), `rows()` and `noticed()` for the notebook, `sparkle(id)`, `fresh()` state, `start`, `intro`, `blurb`, `track` and `next`. Overlays the game draws on a wall (a timetable, three stripes) are `overlays:{view: fn}`.
- A stage may also give `begin(then)` (4j), `onRender(view)` (set the scene's mood on every render: `school3` toggles the scene's `dark` class while the blind is down) and `supersedes:'<key>'` (a version built beside the stage it will replace; the audit lets the two share hotspot ids). A stage with five or more trails gets a two-column notebook; its Noticed shows the newest four still in use.
- A stage ends with `showEnd(title, text, small)`; the card offers "Play this stage again" and the way on to `next`. Stages run on one save, keyed by build.
- Items can be used on each other in the strip: hold one, tap the other; `USES['a>item:b']` says what happens (the paper on the kaya, the tape on the parcel). Both orders must be listed.
- A person can give a clue (Mum's tip), but only once the player has met the problem it solves, so the line lands as an answer and not as a spoiler.
- A stage's optional hunt (the six paper moths) uses `secret:true` hotspots, which the long-press reveal leaves out.
- Nothing is shared between the three children's stories: no clue, item or hiding place appears in two of them (owner, 29 Sept 2026).



## 4e. Start-up, title and choosing a story (owner, 30 Sept 2026)

- The game opens with the studio start-up: OG Entertainment, drawn by the engine (a moth crosses the dark and lights the name), about four seconds, tap to skip, a plain fade under reduce-motion. Then the title screen: our own moth-and-leaf pattern on deep teal, "The Moth House", Continue and New game, a sound button, and "About this game" with the fiction notice. Nothing on these screens is borrowed from another game; the owner's screenshots were for the idea only.
- Continue picks up the last story played (it is greyed out until there is something to continue). New game goes to "Whose story?": three cards, Mary, Elliot and Zaina, with their age and a line each; a card whose stages are not built yet says "Coming soon" and cannot be chosen; a card with progress warns that it starts again from the beginning.
- Saves are one per child (`mothhouse.landscape.<build>.<child>`), with a pointer to the last child played. The in-game menu has "Title screen".
- **The lite release** (owner, 17 Oct 2026): `LITE` in the engine lists the stages playable from the title (`school`, `packing`). A child whose first stage is not listed is greyed out as "Coming soon" on the choose screen and in the Family tab ("In the full game"), and `switchChild` refuses them, so no Meanwhile switch leads into an unreleased stage. Built in 2026-10-17a. A stage's end card leads into its `next` only when that stage is in `LITE` too (`nextStage`); otherwise the button reads "Coming soon… back to the title", so packing day's card does not open the flight in the lite game (2026-10-18b, when the flight, Nana and Jedi's and Trent Park came into this line).
- `?stage=<key>` in the address still jumps straight into any stage for testing, released or not, skipping the start-up and title.

- The children's cards carry two portraits each: the resting face, and the lit face that fades in on hover, focus or press (log 53).



## 4f. A stage's frame (owner, 30 Sept 2026: "finalise the game feel")

- A stage opens on a title card over its first room: where and when in small capitals, the stage's name, one or two lines of set-up, Begin (a tap anywhere or Enter also begins). It shows only on a fresh start, never on resume. `card:{place, text}` in the stage.
- A stage ends with a beat, then the postcard home: a chime, the moth crosses the room, and a picture postcard arrives with Mary's own recap of the day in her words ("Dear Mum and Dad…"), the stage's picture on the left, her portrait as the stamp, the optional challenge as a P.S. The stage gives `postcard:{pic}` and the text through `showEnd(title, text, ps)`; the engine draws the card and the way on ("Go home and pack", or "To be continued… back to the title" when the next stage is not built).
- Postcard pictures are generated 3:2 in the game's style, saved 900×600 in `next/art/postcards/`; they never carry text.
- Never reuse the scene's class names inside a card (`.msg` is the message line); a clash made the postcard's writing dark and centred.



## 4g. The cog (30 Sept 2026)

- A cog sits top-right on the title screen and in every room and opens one panel with three tabs: Settings (music and sound effects as separate sliders, full screen, show what I can tap), Help (how to play in four lines, back to the title, start this stage again, start the story again) and About (what the game is, the fiction notice, the studio credit). Tap outside, ×, or Escape closes it. The room-only rows hide on the title.
- Anything that wipes progress asks first, inside the panel ("Yes, start again" / "No, keep going"). Never a bare wipe.
- Volumes are 0–10 and kept on the device (`mothhouse.vol`); effects run through one gain node so a slider takes effect at once; music at 0 stops the track and any other value restarts it. The old on/off switch is read once and turned into the two volumes.
- The build id and sound state still appear only with `?debug`, in the panel's corner.



## 4h. The moth's voice (30 Sept 2026)

- One voice for the whole game, all three stories: the moth. It speaks the story-led lines only: the stage cards and intro, every note and letter when it opens, every hint in the notebook, the notebook's "that's everything", and the postcard home. Tap lines ("a mug, cold tea") are never voiced, except in Zaina's stage, where everything shown is spoken because she cannot read (4k).
- Lines are recorded once, at build time, with Google's speech model through the Gemini key (`gemini-2.5-flash-preview-tts`, voice Vindemiatrix, with a fixed style instruction: soft, warm, unhurried, a little whispery, British English), encoded to 48 kbps mono mp3 in `next/art/voice/<key>.mp3`. Nothing is generated while a child plays. The scripts are `extract-lines.js` (pulls the lines out of the stage files by key: `<stage>.hint.<i>.<n>`, `<stage>.note.<key>`, `<stage>.card`, `<stage>.intro`, `<stage>.postcard`, `nb.done`, `nb.that`) and `voice.py` (records only lines whose text changed) in the session scratchpad; keep copies with the repo's tools when the scratchpad is lost.
- Changing a voiced line means re-recording it: run the two scripts after editing a stage. The speech model allows 10 requests a minute and 100 a day on our key (30 Sept 2026: the daily cap is per model, separate from credits, so Elliot's and Zaina's 79 lines were recorded on `gemini-2.5-pro-preview-tts` the same evening with the same voice name; Mary's last 22 and the six tips follow on the flash model when its day resets. If the owner hears a difference between the two models, re-record the 79 on the flash model over two days). `next/art/voice/index.json` lists the recorded lines; the engine speaks only those, so an unrecorded line is silent rather than an error.
- The engine plays a line by key (`speak`), one at a time; the moth appears beside the message line while it speaks and settles when done; closing a note or the notebook hushes it. "The moth's voice" has its own slider in Settings; at 0 the game is text only.
- The moth is drawn in two poses: front-on (the portrait) and three-quarter with its wings half raised (speaking). Never cute-babyish; calm, wise, warm.
- The name is still the owner's to decide; no recorded line says it.



## 4i. Opening a stage (30 Sept 2026)

- A stage or a room never opens on a blank. The loading screen is the moth, "Opening your classroom…" and a bar that fills as pictures arrive. Only the first view's wall and cut-outs are waited for; the rest of the room, then the other rooms, load quietly behind, so a stage opens in a second or two on a good connection instead of after the whole 9 MB.
- Turning to a wall that has not arrived yet fades it in when it does; cut-outs draw themselves as they land.



## 4j. First-time tips and drag moments (30 Sept 2026)

- The moth says five tips once per device, each at the first moment it is useful and never over a note, the menu, a card or another line: tap and the arrows (a few seconds into the first room), the strip (first thing picked up), holding (first thing held), combining (two usable things in the strip), and the notebook and the long press (a minute or so in, unless the notebook has already been opened). `TIPS` in the engine; spoken as `tip.<name>`.
- A drag moment is a close-up the player must pull something across before the story goes on (the owner's curtain idea from the games he plays): `openDrag({pic, tab, path, …})` draws the picture, a pull tab and the pulled part behind it; the tab must be taken hold of near where it sits, progress can only creep forward, a stray tap gets a line, letting go past the end finishes with a sound. The first is the suitcase zip at the end of packing day (the path follows the zip round the lid's edge, three points measured on the grid). Blinds, curtains and bolts later use the same call.
- Drag paths are polylines in picture percentages; the tab's box keeps the cut-out's proportions like any other cut-out.
- Where a thing is worked by hand, the close-up asks for the real movement and the movement is the answer (turn, slide, lift-then-pull, press-and-hold), and it sits beside the room rather than over it, so the player stays in the child's shoes (owner, 30 Sept 2026; backlog item 12). A plain look stays a tap; a gesture is never asked for its own sake.
- The first of these is Dad's desk drawer (build 2026-09-30l): `openAside(inner, side)` puts a close-up beside the room with the room left sharp and the arrows hidden, on the side away from the thing tapped (`sideAwayFrom(h)`); `openLiftPull({bg, front, inside, lift, pull, stuck, done})` moves a cut-out front over the empty cavity, with what is inside showing as it comes out. A straight pull jams and springs back (and marks the drawer stuck, so Mum's tip lands); lifting a finger's width and then pulling, still holding, brings it out. A player who lifts before asking Mum is simply right. The front is cut from the close-up itself and the cavity painted behind it, so no new picture was needed. Once emptied, the drawer slides shut and open again on a tap (the first of backlog 15).
- A stage's opening gesture is its `begin(then)`: the engine runs it once, after the title card and before the first line, on the save's own flag (`begun`), never on resume (built 17 Oct 2026 for Mary's version 3: the blind cord, a drag on the window wall). Every stage should open with one such gesture, before the first line and before any puzzle, and it should mean something in the story: waking, letting the light in, opening the way (owner, 30 Sept 2026; backlog item 11 lists the one chosen for each stage). The gesture is never a test: a wrong direction or an early release simply waits.



## 4k. Elliot's and Zaina's first stages: a stage for each age (30 Sept 2026)

- Every child's story now starts in the landscape engine: `eschool` (Elliot, Classroom 2P and the playground) and `zschool` (Zaina, the party room at Little Waves). A stage says whose it is with `child:'elliot'`, so `?stage=eschool` opens as that child; the notebook, the postcard's stamp and the "start the story again" wipe all follow the child being played.
- **Age 7 (Elliot):** two rooms, two trails open at once, a hands-on puzzle you can see working. The marble run is a close-up with three big gaps; a held piece goes into the gap you tap, a wrong gap says why it does not fit ("the marble would shoot straight off the edge") and sends you to the picture clue. The engine puts a used item down before the stage sees it, so `openRunWith(piece)` picks it up again for the close-up. Roll animates the marble down the whole run and rings the bell (chimes). The hint text is short and plain; the notebook shows the two trails and the cogs count.
- **Age 4 (Zaina):** one room, no doors, nothing to read. Her mark is a yellow star, on her cubby beside her portrait, and every task is "find the one with the star" or "copy the pictures". `hintStyle:'point'`: the moth button and Peanut do not open a notebook; the game turns to the right wall, the moth flies from its corner to the thing, the thing glows and the moth says one line (`pointNext`, hints carry `at:{room, view, id}` and a single tip). **Every line in her stage is spoken**: tap lines go through `line(key, text)` (key `zschool.line.<name>`, listed in the stage's `lines` map so the extractor records them), wrong uses through the stage's `miss(item, id)`. The four instruments each make their sound; a wrong one is never a failure, just "not that one yet".
- The picture rule again: what is on a wall is on its close-up. The peg name cards and the cubby marks are drawn by the game (SVG overlays, `overlays:{p3:…}`) on the wall and in the close-up from one list; the bottles and paintings that are not Zaina's were composited onto the walls from the close-ups, and her own bottle and painting are cut-outs that leave the wall when taken and appear inside her cubby when put away.
- When the Gemini credits ran out mid-build (HTTP 402, 30 Sept 2026) the last two close-ups (the star bottle on the shelf, the rocket jumper in the box) and both postcards were composited from art already made, and both stages use the classroom track. The key was topped up the same evening: the two close-ups, both postcards and a Lyria track each (`eschool.mp3`, `zschool.mp3`) were then generated properly, and the voice lines recorded.



## 4l. Switching child mid-game: "Meanwhile…" (owner, 30 Sept 2026)

- The three stories are the same afternoon, so a switch is the camera moving to another child, not a menu. The cog's first tab, **Family**, shows the three portraits with where each one is ("Packing day, in the kitchen", "Not started yet", "Playing now"). Tap another child: the moth flies off across the room, the room fades, the loading bar says "Meanwhile, the party room…", and a MEANWHILE card names the child with the stage's one fixed line about their day (`meanwhile` in the stage, voiced as `<stage>.meanwhile`, written so it is true at any point of the stage). Go on lands in their room where they left it, or on their first day if they have not started.

## 4z. Corners that agree: the engine paints the side walls (owner, 17 Oct 2026)

- A wall picture is a flat back wall; its two side walls are the engine's, painted from the neighbouring views so the corners always agree: the left side wall from the near end of the *previous* view (its right-hand end, which meets this wall at the corner), the right side wall from the near end of the *next* view (its left-hand end). Whatever stands at the end of a wall, picture and cut-outs alike, appears foreshortened in the corner of the view beside it, and a change to one wall ripples to its neighbours for free (`drawSides` in the engine; backlog 19).
- How it is drawn: one screen column at a time, each column a thin slice of the neighbour's back wall, the slice position running with depth (1/height along the trapezoid, so the far end is squeezed and the edges stay straight); the neighbour's cut-outs at rest are sliced the same way (a thing in a pocket shows only above its rim); the corner falls into a soft shade so the join reads as a fold. The picture's own ink line at the corner is left showing (an inset of a quarter of a percent).
- What a stage gives: `sides:{<view>:{back:[xL, xR, yT, yB], l:{x, yT, yB, yT0, yB0}, r:{…}, depth?}}` in picture percentages: `back` is the view's own back-wall rectangle (the source when this view is someone's neighbour); `l` and `r` are the side-wall trapezoids this view shows (inner edge at `x` with the corners `yT`..`yB`, outer edge at the picture's edge with `yT0`..`yB0`). `depth` is how much of the neighbour's wall shows (default from the side wall's width, 8–35%). Measured once per wall from the ink lines on a 1% grid (`tools/sides.py` proposes them; check the quads drawn on the picture before trusting them). A view without `sides` keeps its baked corners, which fail checklist 46.
- For walls already made, the engine paints over the baked side walls, so no hotspot or cut-out percentage changes. New walls are prompted **flat**: "the back wall of the room, straight on, filling the picture, no side walls, no ceiling or floor in perspective"; the engine draws the box. This ends the "shallow box with narrow side walls" wording in section 2 for new art; the ceiling strip and floor band still come from the picture for now.
- Known limit: the perspective of the painted side wall follows the baked trapezoid, and some generated walls drew their side panels almost flat (Mary's timetable wall), so there the corner shows a wide, lightly foreshortened piece of the neighbour. Acceptable, and it goes away as walls are regenerated flat with engine-drawn boxes.
- **Judged by the owner on 17 Oct 2026 (build 2026-10-17b, Mary's classroom): "looks really weird", and he is right.** Two causes. (1) The baked side panels of the generated walls are not in perspective (the timetable wall's are flat rectangles a fifth of the picture wide), so a neighbour painted onto them is a wide, barely foreshortened picture pasted beside the room, not a fold. (2) The walls of one room disagree with each other: the mint band sits at mid-height on the timetable wall and far lower on the whiteboard wall, ceilings and floor lines differ, the creams differ; no corner can join lines that do not meet. So: **painting over baked corners is switched off** (no stage gives `sides`; the code stays, dormant, in `drawSides`). The fix that holds is the one the visual style always said: walls generated **flat under one room specification** (one wall colour, one band height, one skirting and ceiling line, one floor, written down per room before any prompt), and the engine drawing the whole box, ceiling, floor and side walls, in one perspective it controls. That is a regeneration of a room's walls, so it goes through Gate D with an estimate; it is noted in the school level file. Until then the baked corners stay and checklist 46 is a known fault.
- The classroom's measured boxes (for when its walls are redone) are in `levels/LEVEL-school.md`, Notes.



## 4aa. Walls from a room specification: what the image-model evaluation taught (owner, 21 Oct 2026)

Fifty-six pictures of Classroom 5H in one day, 33 models, five rounds, every picture marked by the owner and scored against `evals/images/CRITERIA.md`; the faults and their answers are `evals/images/prompts/GOTCHAS.md` (26 lines). What stands:

- **The model.** GPT Image 2.5 Sunburst draws a room a player can turn round in: four walls, four corners that meet, one light, one band height (owner: 9/10 for consistency, "not my favourite style but very good"). Nano Banana 2 holds a room too, in the flatter hand the owner marks down; it is the fallback. Seedream 5.0 Pro lights beautifully and changes an object's size across a corner. Qwen 2.1 Pro draws the loveliest single wall and loses it when made to obey; it may yet suit postcards, comic pages and close-ups, which need not line up with anything.
- **The method.** One room specification names the palette, the band height, the single light source and every object on every wall with its position and size. Scripts build the four prompts and four wordless layout sketches from it, so no wall can disagree with another. Each prompt: frame and style paragraphs, the room block, a numbered object list in thirds, what each side wall shows (the neighbour's end, named, and what is out of view), this wall's light, a checks line. The anchor wall (the busiest) is drawn first with its sketch; its neighbours next with the anchor attached; the opposite wall last with all three. References go in `input_references`; a sketch carries nothing written, not even numbers.
- **What the game draws, not the model.** Anything a puzzle reads: the clock (ask for a blank face), the timetable grid (ask for an empty grid under one row of icons), labels beyond two or three short words. Fourteen of thirty-one models drew the wrong time; most mangled the grid.
- **Corners.** All four finalists drew meeting corners from words alone: the neighbour's end on each side wall, foreshortened, with light from the room's one window. This is the "corners that agree" problem (4z) answered in the art for a pair and a room; the engine's side walls remain the guaranteed fix and the two can coexist.
- **Costs.** About four cents a wall on Sunburst, ten on Nano Banana 2; the day's 56 pictures cost $5.58. Every run's real charge is in `evals/images/results/`.



**Decided by the owner, 24 Oct 2026** (after the test room's polish walk, checklist 43): **every future level of The Moth House uses the test room's design and art style.** The art is the room-specification method (this section) on GPT Image 2.5 Sunburst, the style signed off on 21 Oct 2026 (section 2): rendered and lit, clean ink lines, soft shading, a paper grain, with close-ups and icons made to the same standard (the test room `levels/LEVEL-evalroom.md` is the reference, kept for that). The design is the test room's way of playing: close-ups that take items, finding as a close-up, drawers that open and shut and are looked into, one thing one thing, a key that is a tap (4ac, 4ad, checklist 59 to 67). The finished levels are not re-made now; they stay as they are until each is rebuilt. **The owner rebuilds the level logic and the puzzle maps himself**: every level, the existing ones included, starts again from his brief and his map through the gates, and nothing of the old stages' puzzle logic is kept without his new map. The old brief's points that do not conflict stand (landscape, the strip, close-ups over the softened room, the arrows, micro-animations).

## 4ab. Micro-animation, planned from the outline (owner, 21 Oct 2026)

The game should feel alive the way the Rusty Lake and Dark Dome games do: still rooms, a little constant life, a short movement on every tap, and a reward moment on every solve. Section 4 says how things move; this says when that is decided and what the art must be made as. The plan behind it, with what the engine already has and what it lacks, is `next/MOTION.md`.

- **Movement is decided at Gate B, on the level's motion sheet**, before any art. Every thing that will move, in any way, is listed with when it moves (idle, tap or solve), the movement, and the art it needs. A thing not on the sheet is painted into its wall and never moves. A wall regenerated to cut something out of it is a re-do and goes in the ledger with its reason.
- **Every solve has a reward moment**: two or three seconds where the lock reacts, the thing opens by its own movement, and what was inside shows, with sound. Then the room settles into its new state and stays that way (checklist 27, 32e). Moments are listed on the motion sheet, step by step. Until backlog 20 is built, a moment is one movement on one group.
- **Living things per view stay one or two** (checklist 26). The sheet makes that easy to see: count the idle rows per view.
- **What the art is made as, by movement type:**
  - *Single movement* (bend, swing, lift…): one cut-out, the way section 2 already says.
  - *Hinged parts* (a person's head, jaw or arm; a lid; a jack-in-the-box): generate the whole figure first as a cut-out sheet (4u). Then generate each moving part alone on magenta, using the whole figure as the reference ("only the head and the top of the neck, same size and place, everything else flat magenta"), and the body without that part ("the neck ends in the collar"). Each part keeps a small overlap at its joint so no gap shows when it turns. Note each joint's pivot point in picture percentages on the sheet.
  - *Pose swap* (wings open and closed, an eye blinking): generate pose A, then make pose B as an edit of pose A ("the same picture, only the wings folded"), so nothing else shifts. Two or three poses at most, all the same size.
  - *Code-drawn* (light, glow, dust in a sunbeam, rain, steam): no art at all.
- **Code before credit.** If a movement can be made by code from art that already exists, that is the choice. New art is only made for a movement when code cannot fake it believably.
- **Child-safe life.** Living things are cosy: a hamster breathing, a lamp's glow, bunting stirring, the moth fluttering. No flames, nothing that lunges, nothing that startles (CLAUDE.md working rules). The grown-up layer gets its uncanny touches from strangeness, never fright: a clock running backwards, a painting that is different when you turn back to it.
- **Reduced motion.** Idle loops rest; taps and moments still play, shorter and gentler (section 4).
- **The engine is frozen while a level is between Gate A and release**, so the movements the sheet asks for that the engine cannot yet do go to the backlog (items 20 to 23 in section 8) and are built between levels, then tested across every level. School version 3 (`school3`) was mid-gates when this was written: it takes the motion sheet from its next gate onward and gets no new movements until it is released.



## 4ac. The first playable replaces the greybox (owner, 22 Oct 2026)

The owner asked whether the greybox still applies "in an AI-native world". The answer agreed: the gate stays, the greyness goes.

- **Why the greybox existed.** In a studio, art is the expensive part, so the puzzle is proved with boxes before an artist spends a week. At a few cents a wall that reason is gone. But this project's rules were never about credit. They came from the pain cheap art caused: walls regenerated to cut a moving thing out and no longer matching their neighbours (checklist 50, 53), cut-outs re-lifted and re-measured, voice lines re-recorded after a line changed (gate D). A wrong puzzle after the art is a cascade, and the cascade is what the gate before the art prevents.
- **What changed.** The scarce thing is the owner's evening on the tablet, not an artist's time. A grey room spends that test on logic alone, when he also needs to judge feel, and no one can tell whether a room is at Rusty Lake standard from boxes.
- **The rule now.** Gate C stays "it plays right, on the tablet, before the script locks and before any voice or batch". What is played at that gate is a **first playable**: real walls and cut-outs wherever they exist, real logic, and plain stand-ins only for the pictures that do not exist yet. A stand-in is a labelled box, or a crop lifted from an existing wall by the 4r method (a drawer front, a door leaf, the things in a box). The two cheap, reversible things are allowed before the gate; the two expensive ones wait behind it: no voice is recorded, and no wall is redone, until Gate C is signed. A wall that does not exist yet may be generated before the gate only when the owner has agreed its cost and accepts that a puzzle change can send it back; the default is to wait.
- **The motion sheet keeps its place at Gate B**, because a wall still has to be prompted with its moving things left off (4ab), and that cannot be undone after.
- **Code before credit still holds**: a stand-in that the game can draw (a date on a board, a grid, a keyhole, a row of pots) is drawn, and that drawing often becomes the finished thing (checklist 52).
- First use: the evaluation room's "Lock-up Duty" (levels/LEVEL-evalroom.md), build 2026-10-22b: four existing walls and nine cut-outs, five more lifted from the walls by crop, stand-ins for two keys, a ruler, a torn note and the pot close-up.

## 4ad. Close-ups take items, and finding is a close-up (owner, 22 Oct 2026, from the first tablet play of the test room)

Two faults with one root, found in the first first-playable (MOTH-49, MOTH-50, MOTH-51).

- **What takes an item in the room takes it in its close-up.** The small key opened the cabinet from the room but not inside the cabinet close-up, where the keyhole is; the ruler would not measure the plants inside the pot close-up, so the owner "clicked around" until he tried the sill from the room. The engine only routes a held item through the room's hotspots; a close-up card had no target. Rule: every close-up of a thing that takes an item carries its own target over that thing (the stage's `cuTarget` and `wireCu` in the test room: a transparent button with the room hotspot's id, so `USES` is the same table), a plain tap on it says the line, and a wrong item gets the same friendly miss as in the room. Backlog 25 moves this into the engine so a stage declares it once.
- **Finding is a close-up.** The half note came out of the bin from the room view on one tap, and the corner under the hat was a small wall target. The owner: "when finding new items like the notes I think it would be a better experience to see a zoomed in version of that view". Rule: searching a place plays its movement (the lid flips, the box is bumped), then the place opens close up, made from the wall itself (`zoom`, a region of the wall filling the 16:9 card, no new art), and the found thing is seen and taken there at a finger's size. It leaves the close-up with the pickup sound and goes to the strip. The cabinet, the desk drawer, the bin and the lost property box all work this way now; the envelope on the desk, which is in plain sight, is picked up where it lies.
- **The close-up looks where a child would look, and it is its own picture.** The owner, on the wall-zoom close-ups (build 2026-10-22c): "it needs new visuals… inside the bin taken from the top perhaps with a mini puzzle to move the trash around to find the note… a close up of the inside of the hat". A zoom of the wall shows the outside of a thing; finding happens inside it. So the close-up of a bin looks down into it, the close-up of a hat looks into the hat, and each is a picture of its own at Gate D (the empty bin from above, the rubbish as cut-outs, the hat's inside). Until then a code-drawn stand-in plays (build 2026-10-22d: `openBin`, `openHat`). Searching inside is a small puzzle of its own where it suits: the rubbish is pushed aside piece by piece and the note is under the last (a tap shoves; dragging the pieces is backlog 27).
- **What opens, shuts.** "The drawers need open and shut mechanics" (owner, same play). An unlocked drawer slides open on a tap and shut on the next, in the room and in its close-up alike, with what is inside showing only while it is out; the contents are taken from the close-up. The engine has slide-open only, so the stage draws an unlocked drawer itself with a short transition (`wallDrawer`, `toggleDrawer`, `drawerHTML` in the test room) until backlog 26 puts the shut movement and the open/shut rest states in the engine. The first opening is still the engine's reward movement.
- **From the owner's spoken commentary (22 Oct 2026, the first recording with sound; log 66).** Four more rules, each from a remark pinned to its frame: a tap that zooms does not also animate, and a thing with nothing in it does not animate at all (MOTH-58); a drawing over a target never takes the tap (MOTH-60); an open drawer zooms to itself, and a lock belongs to its drawer, not the whole desk (MOTH-61); one line per screen (MOTH-68). And one about the wall art: when a poster's grid cannot hold what the puzzle needs (four rows for five days), the game paints the grid itself (MOTH-56, checklist 52).
- **From the fourth recording (24 Oct 2026; log 68).** Four more: *one thing is one thing*: a tool the player picked up once is drawn once, and when it is used in several places it moves between them, never copies itself (the ruler, MOTH-77); *a close-up's pictures load with the room*: a stage lists them in `extra`, so a card never opens half drawn (MOTH-76); *the title is drawn last* in a close-up, over the drawer and the targets, as the engine's own cards do, and *a cavity is solid*: the gap behind an open drawer is painted opaque, never a tint over the wall (MOTH-78); *a sample's surround is part of the sample*: the hat passed as a cap and failed as a picture, because it lay on wood inside a cardboard box, so a close-up's prompt names what the thing lies on and what is around it, from the room (MOTH-79).
- **From the fifth recording (24 Oct 2026; log 70).** *A lock belongs to the thing it locks and moves with it*: the keyhole is drawn on the drawer front, in every state, never as a separate overlay that can linger or vanish (MOTH-84). *What is inside speaks; shutting is its own action*: in a look-down close-up a tap on a thing inside says its line, and the drawer shuts from a button, never from a tap that might have meant "look at that" (MOTH-89). *Show one reading, not the answer sheet*: a measuring tool shows the reading where it stands and nothing is tallied for the player, not in the footer and not in the notebook (MOTH-87); and *a tool that lives somewhere leaves the strip* (MOTH-88). *Measure overlays from pixels*: a label or slot placed over a picture is measured from the picture's pixels (a white-region search or a fine grid on the band), not read off a 5% grid by eye (MOTH-83). *Key the rim*: a cut-out's edge pixels take their alpha from their whiteness, or a white fringe shows on every dark surface (MOTH-85).
- **A thing that turns on the spot is turned by the angle swept, not by a path; and a key in a door is a tap** (24 Oct 2026; MOTH-93; logs 71 and 72). The engine's `openDrag` moves a tab along a path under the finger: right for a zip or a bolt, wrong for a key, a dial or a tap, where the finger goes round and the thing only rotates. For a dial or a tap the stage reads the angle from the pivot to the finger and turns the picture by the sweep (backlog 29 for the engine). For a key in a door even that is too much: the owner's finger completed the sweep and he still said it did not work, because the key never looked inside the lock and the turn was hard to see. So a key is drawn from the side with its shank going into the keyhole, it slides in along the shank, and one tap turns it over about the shank's own line, in perspective, the bow going edge-on and round (logs 72 to 74: a lever swing and a clock-hand spin round the keyhole both read wrong to the owner at once; a key turns about its shank, and the shank is horizontal). The picture being right is the feel; the scripted play taps and waits, as a player would.

## 4ae. The owner's recorded feedback: how a round is reviewed (22 to 24 Oct 2026)

Seven rounds of the test room were reviewed from the owner's tablet recordings with his voice on them (logs 65 to 74). The way of doing it, so every later level gets the same:

- **How it arrives.** He records the tablet's screen with his commentary. A file over the chat's limit goes to his Drive, shared "anyone with the link"; it is downloaded with `curl -sL -c cj.txt -b cj.txt "https://drive.usercontent.google.com/download?id=<id>&export=download&confirm=t"`. A smaller one is uploaded into the session. Either way it goes into its own empty folder in the scratchpad.
- **Transcribe and sheet.** `python3 tools/transcribe.py <recording> <out-dir>`: the sound track through OpenRouter (a few cents), his words with a time per line, and contact sheets at a frame every two seconds so each remark is pinned to what was on screen. Read the transcript first, then only the sheets the remarks point at; a remark that names a bug is checked against its frames before anything is built.
- **Log first.** Every point is one Jira item (the game-feedback-jira skill: one problem or idea each, his words quoted with the time, the file and function named under Where, a repeat of an open card as a comment on it, never bundled), before a line of code. Praise is kept too, in the feedback log, because it says what to keep.
- **Build, then classify.** Each fix goes in the same round and is classified before the commit: a rule for every level goes into this playbook (4ad and its neighbours), into the checklist, and into the audit when a machine can check it; a rule for this level only goes into the level file's learning loop. The build's cards each get a comment naming the build, the branch, the commit and the play link; what was not built says why on its card (an engine change waits for between levels; a picture waits for its gate; a design question is his).
- **Reply in his shape.** What was fixed, what was not and why, the records, one next step, the fresh link. One step at a time: never a list of things for him to do.
- **Two lessons from the key.** When the same point comes back twice, the design changes, not the tuning (the key turn went from a path drag to a sweep to a tap before the picture was right). And a gesture that fails twice on the tablet becomes a tap and an animation the player watches: the picture being right is the feel. A scripted play passing is not evidence a gesture works; the script must drive it as a finger would, and a finger is the owner's.

## 5. Sound and music

- Effects are made in the browser (Web Audio) and must be loud enough for a tablet speaker: taps 0.3, knocks 0.4, chimes 0.25. They start on the first real tap.
- Music is recorded tracks made with Google's Lyria model through the same Gemini key (`art/music/README.md`): one for the title screen and one per stage (`track` in the stage). The engine keeps one wanted track (`setTrack`) and cross-fades when it changes: title → school → flat → title, never a cut. Random notes are not music; the built-in composed waltz is only a fallback if a file fails.
- Ambient sound is made in the browser and named by the stage (`ambient`): rain with the odd drip for the flat, distant birds and a far-off party for the school. It sits under the music slider, quietly, and starts with the music after the first tap.
- A recorded track needs a *completed* tap (`touchend`/`click`), not finger-down, before the browser allows it. If refused, keep nothing and retry on the next tap. Report the state in the menu. This was why the owner heard effects but no music.

## 7. Lessons from the genre's reviews (owner's research, 29 Sept 2026)

Distilled from player and critic reviews of Rusty Lake, Machinarium, Samorost, Day of the Tentacle, Monkey Island, Grim Fandango, Broken Sword, Agent A, Dark Dome and Forgotten Hill. What we adopt, and where it now lives:

- **Several puzzles open at once**, in any order, after a short linear opening that teaches the mechanic. → Section 4c; the classroom is the linear opening, the corridor is the hub.
- **Fair puzzles**: every step follows from something the game has shown; the player knows *why* as well as *what*. For every lock, write down the in-game clues that point to its answer before building it. → Each trail's note says what to look for and why; nothing needs outside knowledge.
- **Tiered hints the player chooses**: nudge, then what to try, then the answer, for the puzzle they pick, with instant access and no gatekeeping. → Mary's notebook (the moth button, or Nutmeg): a to-do list of the riddles with how far each has got, tap one for that trail's hint; three taps reach the answer. **No sparkles by default** (owner, 17 Oct 2026): a hint row names its `target`, the second tier makes that thing pulse for a few seconds once the notebook closes, the long-press reveal stays as accessibility. Version 3 of Mary's school day is built this way; the older stages still sparkle until they are revisited.
- **An in-game notebook records clues** so nobody has to write things down or photograph the screen. → The notebook's "Noticed" line records what has been seen; and from 18 Oct 2026 (owner: "other games let you take important clues with you"), **clues travel**: a page, map, banner, board or table read in one room is copied into the notebook the first time it is seen (`copyClue`, `STAGE.clues()`) and reopens from a chip there, anywhere in the level, so no clue is memorised or walked back to. Items in the strip are for things; clues live in the notebook (checklist 47).
- **No pixel-hunting**: challenge comes from thinking, not finding. Big tap targets, a way to reveal hotspots, key things never hidden in clutter. → A long press on the room (or "Show what I can tap" in the menu) glows every tappable thing for a moment; sparse walls by design.
- **Wrong tries teach**: a useful line, never a punishment. → Using the star key on the colour padlock says it has dials, not a keyhole.
- **Minimise backtracking**: compact maps, and once a route or mechanism is solved it stays solved. → Five rooms round one corridor; opened padlocks and drawers stay open; taken slips are never shown again.
- **Never the same puzzle twice**: cap each puzzle type at two per stage. → Number dials (locker) and colour dials (cupboard) are the only pair; sequence, key and observation locks are each used once.
- **Autosave** after every change; animations short enough never to need skipping. → Every render saves; the longest animation is the tumble (2.4 s).
- **One signature mechanic** per game, deepening as it goes. **TO DECIDE** with the owner: what The Moth House's is (the moth itself? clues carried between chapters? the three children's views of one summer?).
- **Two difficulty modes** by adding or removing steps, not changing puzzles: a cheap way to make replay worth it. Noted for later; the Night Moth bonus is the hard layer for now.
- **Post-game secrets** for dedicated players, always solvable in-game. → The Night Moth riddle per chapter.
- Monetisation: never gate hints or progress behind ads. Not relevant yet (no ads planned).



## 7b. What 162,575 player reviews say (owner's research, 17 Oct 2026)

Every English Google Play review (Singapore store) of 41 point-and-click and escape games from Dark Dome, Rusty Lake, Amanita, Yak & Co, Revolution and Devolver, tagged by keyword against 25 themes (the owner's deck "What 162k player reviews tell us about The Moth House"; keyword tagging, one storefront, correlation only, so the small themes are directional). Dark Dome and Rusty Lake are 88% of the data and the closest to us. What it says, and what we do with it:

- **Bugs are the biggest cause of bad reviews** (3% of all reviews, 21% of the 1–3★ ones; lost progress, crashes, soft-locks and black screens lead). → Autosave after every action is already how the engine works. Two things are not: **saves are keyed by build, so every release starts every player again** (`KEY = 'mothhouse.landscape.' + BUILD`), and the game never asks Android for persistent storage, so a save can be evicted under storage pressure. Both are backlog 18, engine work between levels. A soft-lock check (no state the player cannot leave) joins the Gate C tests (checklist 42).
- **Stuck players leave for YouTube and rate lower** (5.5% of reviews needed hints, 13% of the low ones; "unclear direction" averages 3.32★). → Three-tier hints, the notebook and the hotspot reveal already exist (section 7); the Gate B paper playtest checks that every lock has a visible clue. Keep it.
- **AI-looking art is punished when it is inconsistent** (103 reviews, 3.31★, mostly one 2025 game whose generated backgrounds did not match its hand-drawn ones). → One locked style with a reference crop is already the rule (section 2); the sample pack (Gate E) exists for this. New: before a level's final sign-off, its walls, cut-outs and close-ups are looked at **side by side with the finished levels'** so a drift in style, line weight or detail is caught before release (checklist 43).
- **Phones, not tablets** (91.5% of reviews from phones; "UI readability" averages 3.83★: small text, dark scenes, hard-to-read notes). → The checklist's screen shapes already include 740×360; new: each level is played once on a **real mid-range Android phone**, not only the owner's tablet and the Chromebook (checklist 44).
- **Ads are tolerated when the player chooses them** (praise for few ads 4.87★, rewarded ads for hints 4.39★, forced ads 4.19★). → Decided by the owner, 17 Oct 2026: the MVP is a "lite" game of one or two levels with no ads; the full version ships later with ads (`STORY.md`, open decision 4 and the launch plan). Nothing in the engine assumes ads until then. If ads ever come, the rule from the data is rewarded ads tied to hints only, plus a one-off remove-ads purchase, never interstitials between scenes.
- **Story and atmosphere are what players praise most** (1 in 4 reviews, 4.8★; "wants more" is the highest-rated theme at 4.93★). → The comic intros, the moth's voice, the postcards home and one connected summer across three stories are the right bets; keep every stage's story beat (section 4f) and plan the series (CLAUDE.md, "Longer term").
