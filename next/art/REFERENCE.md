# Reference notes: what the real places look like, in words

The owner sends photos of real places so the game's rooms feel true. The photos are never kept in the repo (checklist A4): what stays is this file, in words, with everything that must not be copied marked. Prompts for new walls draw on these notes at Gate D; nothing here is a licence to generate anything before a level's gates are signed.

The fiction rule (`STORY.md`, "Fiction and legal safety") applies to every line below: the game's school is **Knightsbrook International School**, never the real one. No real name, crest, logo, uniform, staff name, student name or address goes into a picture, a label or a line. No real person's face is drawn from; the children in the game are the three portraits and the invented teachers.

## Knightsbridge House International School, Singapore (photos from the owner, 17 Oct 2026)

Five photos: a classroom with its teacher and class, a whiteboard wall, a corridor-side office nook, the street entrance, and an assembly in the hall. What they show, generically:

**The building.** One floor of a modern grey office building, reached through glass doors at street level. Inside, an open-plan feel: white walls, grey carpet tiles or pale vinyl floor, and above everything a **dark, exposed ceiling**: black-painted concrete and services, silver air-conditioning ducts and square vents, a ceiling-mounted projector, strip lights. That dark ceiling over white walls is the single most distinctive thing about the place and is not in our walls today (ours have a plain pale ceiling strip).

**The classroom.** Not a primary-school room with little tables but a bright, slightly office-like room: **pale beech-topped desks on black steel legs**, pushed into a U or in rows, **red moulded-plastic chairs**, backpacks dumped on desks. One long wall is almost all **whiteboard**, with the day's date and plan written in marker in the teacher's hand (blue and red pens; a date, a weekday, a list of lessons, a reminder about an event). A **round analogue wall clock** hangs above the board. Laminated A4 and A3 posters are stuck to the walls and above the board: a reward chart, a "voice levels" scale, small illustrated classroom-rule cards, students' written work, a world-map poster, sticky notes. A **black pinboard** on a side wall. A grey wire basket of books; a drying rack of worksheets; a small shelf with plastic plants and a spray bottle in the corridor nook. UK-style three-pin sockets low on the walls. No lockers in shot (ours are a game device and stay).

**The hall.** A plain long room with a beige floor, used for assembly: the children sit cross-legged on the floor in rows, a **projector screen** at the front shows a slide, a trestle table with a cloth holds certificates, a fire extinguisher on the wall, a few plastic chairs along the side. Certificates are handed out in front of everyone. Our hall's certificates, house points and stage fit this; a projector screen above the stage would fit too.

**Colour.** Red is the school colour everywhere: the uniform (a red polo or hoodie with a white chest band, khaki shorts or skirt, sometimes a red tee with the school name), the chairs, red festival decorations at the entrance for Chinese New Year. **Must not be copied:** the red-and-white band uniform, the crest (a shield with a bird), the name and initials, the tee lettering. Knightsbrook's colour stays undecided; if it is red it must be a different cut (no white band), and the house colours in the game (red, blue, yellow; Kingfishers) are already our own.

**People.** Teachers in plain smart clothes (a navy blouse, a dress), warm and informal with the class; a class of about fourteen ten-year-olds from many backgrounds. For the game: Mr Hollis and Mrs Ng are invented; the children are never shown in the school stages (everyone is at the party), which keeps every face out of the question.

**Words seen on boards (not for copying, only for flavour):** a date and weekday at the top; lesson lists (English, Science, Maths, Mandarin, Art, PE, Break, Lunch); a mid-year assessment timetable; a club notice (a poetry recitation, a spelling bee, a Model United Nations activity); a list of names for a news-report task. Our timetable and whiteboard key already follow this shape. Mandarin as a lesson is a true Singapore detail our timetable lacks (we have Geography and Library in its place); worth a thought when the timetable is next revisited, with the picture key to match.

### What this could change, if the owner wants it (a Gate D decision for whichever level it touches)

1. **The dark exposed ceiling** over white walls, in every school room. The biggest gain in truth for the least change, but it is a style decision across the whole school, and the current walls are deliberately clean and sparse (owner's brief, 28 Sept 2026).
2. **Desks and chairs**: beech tops on black legs, red plastic chairs, instead of the classic wooden school furniture in the classroom walls.
3. **The whiteboard** carrying the day's plan in marker handwriting (drawn by the game, so the words stay exact), with laminated cards above it.
4. **A projector screen** above the hall stage and children's work pinned low on the walls.
5. **Mandarin** on the timetable.

None of this touches Elliot's or Zaina's rooms: Elliot's school is the same building (his classroom could take the same ceiling and furniture), Zaina's is a different kindergarten.
